User guide How a lease-end estimate is built The model estimates a vehicle price from its characteristics; lease inputs are used to construct a projected vehicle state.
01

Describe the vehicle

Vehicle make and model, year, mileage, horsepower, condition, accident history, and available vehicle attributes are prepared as model inputs.

02

Project its lease-end state

Expected annual mileage and time remaining to lease end are used to estimate future mileage and vehicle age. An optional lease-end condition can define the scenario.

03

Estimate market price

The trained vehicle-price model scores that projected profile. It was trained for vehicle market price, not as a time-series model of historical residual values.

04

Compare with the original RV

The estimated market price is compared with the lease's original residual value. The resulting gap indicates potential exposure or value above the original RV.

Metric guide

Reading the scores

MAE · Mean Absolute Error
Average size of the difference between a predicted and actual market price. It treats each error evenly.
RMSE · Root Mean Squared Error
Typical error size with larger misses weighted more heavily than with MAE.
R2 · Coefficient of Determination
How much variation in market prices the model explains on its evaluation data. It is not a percentage accuracy score.

Model assessment

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Uses multiple vehicle characteristics together to estimate market price.
  • Projects mileage and age from explicit lease assumptions before estimating the lease-end vehicle state.
  • Provides an interpretable comparison against the original RV, including the size and direction of the gap.

Limitations

  • Performance scores describe vehicle market-price prediction, not direct lease-end RV or RV-gap accuracy.
  • This is not a time-series forecast trained on earlier market prices to predict later residual values.
  • Market shifts and unobserved vehicle history can affect results; projected mileage and any assumed lease-end condition are scenario inputs.
